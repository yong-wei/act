## Overview

The capability layer adds instructional targets to factual knowledge nodes. A knowledge node can have multiple capability targets, such as remember, understand, apply, analyze, evaluate, or create. These targets must be expressed through course-specific success criteria and evidence requirements.

## Relationships

- Knowledge graph: describes facts and disciplinary relationships.
- Capability layer: describes desired mastery levels on those facts.
- Goal slice: groups capability targets into a planning and diagnosis goal.
- Learner state: materializes observed evidence against those targets.

## Boundary

Knowledge graph edges such as prerequisite or derived-from may inform path planning, but they are not the same as path strategy edges such as remedial, alternative, extension, or fallback.

## Risks

- Treating Bloom levels as the whole competency model would erase existing diagnosis dimensions.
- Writing teacher preferences into knowledge nodes would make factual graph maintenance unstable.
